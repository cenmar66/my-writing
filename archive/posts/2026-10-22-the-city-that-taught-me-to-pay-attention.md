# The City That Taught Me to Pay Attention

*I arrived in Tokyo as an executive with answers. I left as a student with questions. That is the trade.*

Tokyo broke my brain.  In the best possible way.

I arrived as an executive with answers.  I left as a student with questions.  That is the trade.  I would make it again.

#### Arriving Empty

My first months there, I was lonely and overwhelmed.

Divorced.  A small child an ocean away.  A new job in a culture I did not understand.

I had no map.  So I did the only thing left.

I watched.

#### The City as Teacher

I watched a ryokan attendant in Hakone turn room cleaning into art.  The futon.  The tea tray.  The single flower.  Nothing extra.  Nothing missing.

I watched salarymen in dark suits tend tiny gardens.  A few stones.  A patch of moss.  Care given with both hands.

I watched a whole city treat ordinary tasks as sacred. The train on time.  The package folded just so. The bow that meant something.

Not performance. Presence.

#### The Dojo

Then I found a Zen master.

No judgment in him. Only kindness. Steady as a calm sea. After our first meeting, I carried his peace for weeks. Like warmth after sun.

I sat in his dojo. We barely spoke at first.

We just sat.

Some days my mind ran like a river in flood. Some days it was quiet as still water. He never asked which. He only sat with me and corrected my posture.

What he taught me, without much teaching:

Peace is not a place you arrive at.  
It is a way of paying attention.

#### Goals and Moments

Western life had trained me to chase goals.  
Tokyo trained me to inhabit moments.

The tea.  
The breath.  
The step.

Chasing says *later*.  Attention says *now*.  
Chasing lives in the next thing. Attention lives in this one.

Zen mind = Beginner's mind.

I had read those words for years. Tokyo made me live them. I had to become a beginner again to see anything at all.

#### Still Learning

I still lose it.

The old goal-chasing machinery runs deep. The lists. The hurry. The voice that says you are behind.

My daughter has become a woman. Sometimes I still struggle to show how deep my love for her is.

But now I notice when I am gone.  
And I come back sooner.

That is the practice. Not never leaving. Always returning.

#### An Invitation

You do not need a monastery. You do not need Tokyo. You need one ordinary moment, paid full attention.

The cup in your hand. The light on the wall. The person in front of you.  The one you love, near or far.

Start there. Earth School is in session everywhere.

I came to Tokyo with answers. I am still collecting questions.

**What ordinary moment is waiting for you right now?**
