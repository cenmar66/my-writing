# The Word for the Hollow

*Searches for "anhedonia" are up 83% in a year. I find that hopeful. Not the number. The searching.*

There is a word for it now.

Anhedonia. The loss of pleasure in things you used to enjoy.

Searches for the word are up 83% in a year.

I find this hopeful.

Not the number. The searching.

People are trying to name what they feel. Naming comes before facing. Facing comes before changing.

I have sat in rooms where no one had the words. We talked around the hollow feeling for hours.

One honest sentence would have saved us hours.

You can not fix what you can not name.

So name it. Write it down. Say it out loud to someone you trust.

The word is not the wound. The word is the doorway.

Our most talented people are being recruited by the biggest companies in the world to invent ways to remove the friction between our desires and the objects we think will satisfy them.

One tap. One swipe. Same-day delivery.

I spent years on the other side of that table. I watched the dashboards. Time on app. Conversion rate. Days to repeat purchase. Nobody in those meetings asked what happens to a person who never has to wait for anything.

The wanting gets cheap. Then the having gets cheap. Then nothing tastes like much at all.

Now I think I know. Real pleasure needs a little distance. The walk to the store. The letter that takes a week. The meal you cook with your own tired hands. The person we are too scared to approach. Take away all the friction and you take away the wanting. Then the enjoying goes with it.

Pleasure needs friction. Remove the distance and you remove the joy.

What have you been avoiding naming? Where can you add more friction in your own life?
