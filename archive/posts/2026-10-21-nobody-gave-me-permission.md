# Nobody Gave Me Permission

I have worked for a Fortune 500 company and for myself. Only one ever felt like mine.

Ingram Micro taught me scale. I ran a $100 million export division, became Director of Global Sales, and eventually moved to Tokyo as GM of Ingram Micro Japan, overseeing a joint venture with SoftBank.

It was a fine education. It was also someone else’s dream.

Then I wrote the business plan for and launched Latin eVentures and [eAqui.com](http://eAqui.com), an investment-bank-funded B2B e-procurement company operating in five countries. Next came CoachLink, a bootstrapped business and personal coaching network, followed by Sparkle Restoration Services, an emergency services and home remodeling firm. Nobody gave me permission. Nobody handed me a playbook. I made every mistake myself, which meant I actually learned from them.

Here is the part nobody tells you about building your own thing. The freedom is real. So is the fear.

Some months, I didn’t know if payroll would clear. And at eAqui, I learned that the biggest threat to a company can be sitting at its own board table.

In the weeks before a critical board meeting, I finally understood what my partners had been doing. By the time I put it together, I knew exactly how the meeting would go. I would walk into that room as the CEO, and I would walk out fired.

What I didn’t know was that my COO would vote with them.

He was the person I had confided in. I had given him one of my two board seats. My partners had bought him anyway. I only realized it the night before the meeting when my partners took him, a husband and father, to the most expensive brothel in São Paulo. I was there. I watched them buy him. That night, something in me died. Whatever innocence I still had about people and the ways of the world, I left it in that restaurant.

The next morning, his hand went up with theirs.

That is what greed looks like from the inside. It doesn’t announce itself. It sits next to you, and you hand it the chair.

But I would not trade it. When you build it yourself, you can see your fingerprints on every win. Every scar taught you something no seminar could.

The credential that matters is not the title. It is the work. The thing you made with your own hands. The problems you solved for your paying customers.

**Takeaway: **you do not need permission. You need a first customer and the willingness to look foolish.
