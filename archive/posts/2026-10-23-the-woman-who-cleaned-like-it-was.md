# The Woman Who Cleaned Like It Was a Temple

*In Hakone, a ryokan attendant taught me more about mastery than any boardroom ever did.*

In Hakone, I watched a woman turn my hotel room into a work of art. Twice a day.

She was the ryokan attendant. Middle-aged. Gentle hands. A traditional yukata. Her hair in a tidy bun. No hurry in her at all.

Each morning she transformed my tatami room from bedroom to study. Each evening, from study to dining room, then dining room to bedroom. A futon here. A low table there. A small flower in a vase. A corner lamp.

I watched her the way you watch a dancer. Every movement was choreographed. Nothing wasted. Nothing rushed. She used exactly the energy the task required, not one drop more.

She was not cleaning a room. She was practicing something.

Mastery is not a trophy. It is attention, repeated.

The ryokan woman had no audience. No promotion waiting. No one grading her flower placement. She did it beautifully because beautiful was the only way she knew.

Not performance. Presence.

I have tried to live that way ever since. I keep a private motto. Everything I touch becomes beautiful.

Some days I live it. Many days I do not.

But the standard is set. Pay attention. Do the small thing well. Breathe. Repeat.

That is the whole game.

**So let me ask you. What is in front of you right now?**
