# The Chair at 7 PM

*I have never met the morning of transformation. I have met a thousand ordinary evenings in the same chair.*

Every night at 7, I sit in the same chair.

Same lamp. Same notebook. Same cup of tea going cold.

The chair does not care if I am brilliant.

Some nights I write something true. Some nights I write garbage. The chair does not judge. The hour does not judge.

For years I lived by output. Current Projects Board. Monthly numbers. Biweekly Payroll. If it did not produce, it did not survive.

The chair taught me something else.

The practice is not about output. It is about return.

You return to the cushion. You return to the page. You return to the tennis court. Again and again. Especially on the nights you have nothing.

That is where my creative work begins. Not because the chair produces every night. Because it keeps the door open.

People want the breakthrough. The morning they wake up transformed.

I have never met that morning.

I have met a thousand ordinary evenings.

Chair.
Page.
Breath.

It compounds. Quietly. Invisibly. Then one day you look back and barely recognize the person who sat down that first night.

Not performance. Presence.

**Takeaway:** Pick a practice small enough to do daily. Then do it daily. That is the entire instruction manual.

**Where is your chair?**
